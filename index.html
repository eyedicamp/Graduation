Adaptive Gating of EHR and PRS for 5-Year CVD Risk Prediction

Seungyun Shin

Abstract

Cardiovascular disease (CVD) risk prediction can benefit from combining electronic health records (EHR) with genetic information, yet most existing approaches assume that polygenic risk scores (PRS) contribute uniformly across individuals and often place limited emphasis on calibration and clinical decision utility. This thesis investigates an interpretable multimodal framework for 5-year CVD risk prediction in the UK Biobank by adaptively integrating structured EHR features with an 11-component multi-PRS representation. The study was conducted in a White-ancestry subset of 388,746 participants, including 8,900 incident CVD events, where the outcome was defined as the first occurrence of myocardial infarction, stroke, or heart failure within five years after baseline assessment. A two-expert gating architecture was developed to generate separate EHR- and PRS-based predictions and to learn an individual-specific weight reflecting model reliance on genetic risk. Its performance was compared with logistic regression and neural fusion baselines using predefined train/validation splits across multiple seeds and a fixed held-out test set. To obtain reliable risk estimates, seed-wise isotonic calibration was applied on validation predictions and then evaluated through test-time ensembling. Across models, discrimination performance was similar, with AUROC values around 0.78 and the concatenated logistic regression baseline showing the highest mean AUROC among the compared approaches. Although the proposed gating model did not yield a decisive discrimination advantage, it provided interpretable patient-specific estimates of PRS reliance and showed modest, threshold-dependent differences in risk stratification and decision-curve net benefit. Post-hoc isotonic calibration substantially improved calibration metrics, while also contracting predicted probabilities toward the observed event prevalence. In addition, the learned PRS weight tended to be higher in men and to increase with age, suggesting heterogeneity in the relative contribution of genetic information across subgroups. Overall, this thesis shows that adaptive gating offers an interpretable and calibrated approach for integrating EHR and PRS in 5-year CVD risk prediction, while also highlighting the importance of evaluating multimodal prediction models beyond discrimination alone.
